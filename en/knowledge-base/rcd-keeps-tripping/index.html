Your RCD keeps tripping: how to find the cause

If your residual current device (RCD, aardlekschakelaar) keeps tripping, current is leaking away somewhere. The usual culprits are a faulty appliance or moisture. The step-by-step guide below helps you safely identify the circuit and the appliance responsible, without opening anything up.

At a glance

  • The cause is usually a faulty appliance or moisture.
  • Five steps help you find the circuit and the appliance yourself.
  • If it trips even with every circuit switched off, you need an electrician.

What does an RCD do?

An RCD compares the current flowing into your home with the current flowing back. If there is a difference, current is leaking away, for example through a faulty appliance or through a person. The RCD then cuts the power within a fraction of a second.

RCD or circuit breaker?

First check exactly what has tripped.

  • An RCD has a test button. When it trips, several circuits lose power at once.
  • A circuit breaker is a single switch without a test button. When it trips, only that part of the home loses power. The cause is then usually overload: too many appliances on one circuit at the same time.

Step by step: finding the cause

  1. Switch off all circuitsSwitch off every circuit breaker behind the RCD that has tripped.
  2. Switch the RCD back onIf it stays on, continue. If it trips again immediately, see the section further down.
  3. Switch the circuits back on one at a timePause after each one. The circuit that makes the RCD trip is the one to investigate.
  4. Unplug everything on that circuitSwitch that circuit off again and unplug every appliance on it.
  5. Plug the appliances back in one at a timeSwitch the circuit and the RCD back on. The appliance that makes it trip is the cause. Stop using it.

The most common causes

  • A faulty appliance. Especially appliances with a heating element: washing machines, dishwashers, kettles, water heaters and ovens.
  • Moisture. An outdoor light, a garden socket or a connection in the bathroom. If it mainly trips after rain, look outside.
  • A damaged lead, or an extension lead left outdoors.
  • Too many appliances on one RCD. Every appliance leaks a tiny amount of current. Together, many appliances can add up to just too much, without any single one being faulty.

It trips even with every circuit switched off

Then the fault is not in an appliance, but in the fixed installation or in the RCD itself. This is not something you can fix yourself. Leave the RCD switched off and call an electrician.

Never tape it up or bypass it

An RCD that trips is doing its job. Never hold it on with tape or a wedge, and never have it bypassed. The leaking current could then flow through anyone who touches the appliance. That can be fatal.

How to use the test button

Press the test button. The RCD should trip immediately. Then switch it back on. If it does not trip, it is no longer working and must be replaced.

There is no legal requirement for how often you test. Manufacturers' advice ranges from monthly to twice a year. Twice a year is manageable for most households. Bear in mind that the circuits on that RCD will briefly lose power.

When to call an electrician

  • The RCD trips again immediately with every circuit switched off.
  • You can smell burning, or components in the fuse box feel warm.
  • It trips again after every rain shower.
  • It does not respond to the test button.
  • You cannot find the cause using the steps above.
