Single-phase or three-phase: what do I have, and when should I upgrade?

Three things tell you: the label on your meter, the width of the main switch and the connection capacity your grid operator has on record. Upgrading to three-phase is only necessary if what you want to connect no longer fits on single-phase, which is less often the case than many people think.

At a glance

  • The meter label and the main switch show what you have.
  • A lot fits on single-phase. Charging at 11 kW and large batteries need three-phase.
  • The grid operator upgrades the connection; an installer adapts the fuse box.

How to tell what you have

  • The label on the meter. If it says 3x230/400 V, it is a three-phase meter. If it only says 230 V, you have single-phase. Note that a three-phase meter is sometimes fitted on a single-phase connection, so the meter alone is not conclusive.
  • The main switch. On single-phase it is narrow, with two terminals. On three-phase it is roughly twice as wide.
  • The connection capacity. Your grid operator knows exactly what you have, for example 1x35 A or 3x25 A. The first number is the number of phases; the second is the main fuse rating in amps.

Still not sure? Take a photo of the meter and the fuse box. An installer can almost always tell straight away.

What's the difference?

With single-phase, power enters your home over one conductor; with three-phase, over three. With three phases, your home can draw more power in total at the same time. A standard 1x35 A single-phase connection supplies about 8 kilowatts. A 3x25 A three-phase connection supplies about 17 kilowatts.

For comparison: a kettle uses about 2 kilowatts, an oven 3, and an EV charger at full speed 11.

What do you want to connect?

ApplianceWill single-phase usually cope?
Induction hobYes, on its own cooker circuit
EV charger up to about 7.4 kWYes, often with load balancing
EV charger at 11 kWNo, this needs three-phase
Heat pumpOften, depending on the model
Home batterySmaller models yes, high outputs no
Solar panelsSmaller systems yes

It is not about a single appliance, but about what is running at the same time. Load balancing and smart scheduling, such as charging overnight, often avoid the need for an upgrade.

How does an upgrade work?

An upgrade has two parts, carried out by two parties.

The grid operator upgrades the connection. You apply via mijnaansluiting.nl. The grid operator changes the main fuse and, if necessary, the meter and the cable. You pay a one-off fee, which varies by grid operator. Up to and including 3x25 A, most grid operators keep the fixed annual charge the same. In areas where the grid is at capacity, there may be a waiting list for a heavier connection. Your grid operator will tell you if this applies.

An installer adapts the fuse box. The main switch and RCDs are replaced with three-phase versions and the circuits are distributed across the three phases. Without this work, the heavier connection is of no use to you.

In what order should you arrange it?

Start by asking an installer what you actually need. If three-phase turns out to be necessary, you apply to the grid operator and the work on the fuse box is scheduled to match. That way you are not without power for longer than necessary, and you don't pay for an upgrade you didn't need.

When to call an electrician

  • You want to connect an EV charger, hob, heat pump or battery and aren't sure it will fit.
  • The main switch or an RCD trips when a lot is on at once.
  • Your connection has been upgraded, but the fuse box hasn't been adapted yet.
  • The grid operator's main fuse has blown: call 0800-9009.
